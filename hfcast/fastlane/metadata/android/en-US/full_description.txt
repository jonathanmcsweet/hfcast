HFcast tells you which amateur radio bands will reach a place, hour by hour, and
how likely a contact is on each.

The forecast is real VOACAP — the propagation model the Institute for
Telecommunication Sciences built and has maintained for decades — compiled into
the app itself. There is no account, no server and no network request. Put the
phone in aeroplane mode and it still works, because the model, its ionospheric
coefficient maps and a worldwide list of places all ship inside the app.

What it shows:

* A reach map of where the selected band gets to, this hour, in every direction
* Every band at every hour of the day, as reliability rather than a yes or no
* The operating window — lowest and highest usable frequency — through the day
* Your own station: power, mode, and an antenna with its height, gain and
  bearing, all of which change the answer
* A compass showing which way your antenna favours relative to the path

It is climatology with a probability attached, in the way a weather forecast is.
It says what usually happens on a path like yours in a month like this one, not
what will happen this afternoon.

Nothing about the app is tied to Google services. Location comes from Android's
own provider, so it works on a de-Googled phone or an old tablet. Space weather
and searching for places smaller than a city are the only features that use the
network, and both are optional.
